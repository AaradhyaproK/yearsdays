## Understanding Chronological Age Calculation

Calculating someone's exact chronological age seems straightforward on the surface, but Gregorian calendar irregularities make precise determination a nuanced mathematical problem. A calendar year is not an exact integer number of days: a standard common year has 365 days, while a leap year contains 366 days. Furthermore, months vary between 28, 29, 30, and 31 days.

A common pitfall in casual programming is dividing total elapsed milliseconds by 365.25 or 30.4375. While this yields a rough decimal approximation, it regularly produces errors when expressing an age in discrete calendar components (**Years, Months, and Days**).

### The Calendar Borrow Algorithm

Our calculator employs an exact discrete calendar borrow algorithm. To compute the difference between a birth date and a target date:

1. **Days Difference:** We subtract the birth day from the target day (`target.day - birth.day`).
2. **Borrowing Days:** If the target day is less than the birth day, we borrow the number of days in the calendar month immediately preceding the target month.
   - For example, if calculating age on **May 10** for someone born on **May 25**, May (month 5) borrows from **April** (month 4), which has 30 days.
   - The effective target day becomes `10 + 30 = 40`. Subtracting the birth day (`40 - 25`) yields **15 days**.
3. **Months Difference:** Because one month was borrowed, the target month is decremented by 1. We then compute `(target.month - 1) - birth.month`.
4. **Borrowing Months:** If the resulting months value is negative, we borrow 12 months from the target year and decrement the target year by 1.
5. **Years Difference:** We compute `(target.year - 1) - birth.year`.

### Worked Example: Exact Step-by-Step

Let us walk through a real-world scenario:
- **Date of Birth:** March 20, 2000
- **Target Date:** March 15, 2024 (a leap year)

Step 1: Compare days. Target day is 15; birth day is 20. Because `15 < 20`, we borrow the days of the month preceding March 2024, which is **February 2024**. Since 2024 is a leap year, February has **29 days**.
- Borrowed days: `15 + 29 = 44`.
- Elapsed days: `44 - 20 = 24 days`.

Step 2: Decrement target month from March (3) to February (2). Compare months: `2 - 3 = -1`. Because months are negative, we borrow 12 months from the target year.
- Borrowed months: `2 - 1 + 12 = 13 - 3 = 11 months`.

Step 3: Decrement target year from 2024 to 2023. Compare years: `2023 - 2000 = 23 years`.

**Verified Exact Age:** **23 years, 11 months, and 24 days**.

## Age as on a Cutoff Date for Exams and Applications

In government employment, civil service recruitment examinations (such as UPSC, SSC, and state PSC examinations in India, as well as federal civil service bodies in the United States and Commonwealth nations), age eligibility is determined as of an explicit statutory cutoff date rather than the application submission date.

### How Cutoff Dates Function
- **Prescribed Reference Date:** Official exam notifications typically specify that candidates must have completed a minimum age and must not have exceeded a maximum age as of a specific calendar date (for example, August 1 or January 1 of the examination year).
- **Evaluating Completed Years:** Under standard civil service counting conventions, age as on the cutoff date is evaluated on completed full calendar years, months, and days. If a candidate was born on August 2 and the cutoff date is August 1, they have not yet completed that additional year of age on the cutoff date.
- **Official References:** For current recruitment schedules and official guidelines regarding qualifying age cutoffs and age relaxations for reserved categories, candidates should always consult official public portals directly:
  - Union Public Service Commission: [upsc.gov.in](https://upsc.gov.in)
  - Staff Selection Commission: [ssc.gov.in](https://ssc.gov.in)
  - US Office of Personnel Management: [opm.gov](https://www.opm.gov)

## Leap Years and February 29 Birthdays

Individuals born on February 29 during a leap year (often called "leaplings") experience a calendar date that only recurs every four years (with century exceptions). How is their age reckoned during non-leap years?

Across international civil law, two main conventions exist:
1. **The February 28 Convention:** Recognized in numerous administrative contexts and social conventions, this rule considers the person to have completed their yearly cycle on the final day of February. Under this rule, a person born on February 29, 2000 turns 1 year old on February 28, 2001.
2. **The March 1 Convention:** In English statutory law (e.g. *Family Law Reform Act 1969*, section 9), a person attains a given age at the commencement of the relevant anniversary date. In non-leap years, the day following February 28 is March 1. Under this statutory rule, legal majority is attained on March 1.

Our calculator defaults to the **February 28 display rule** for seamless daily progression, while fully documenting both legal traditions.
